Every creative work is built from earlier work. Forms, poses, plots, chord progressions and jokes pass from maker to maker, and each one borrows, recombines and adds a little. Originality isn't making something out of nothing. It's a new arrangement of what already exists.

Nina Paley's animation makes the case without a word. It's made entirely of photographs of centuries-old sculptures at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, sequenced so the figures seem to dance and turn into one another. The film has no imagery of its own, so it proves its point just by existing.

## Why the idea is freeing

The [Lone Genius Myth](/notes/lone-genius-myth/) says great work comes from people with no influences, struck by inspiration out of nowhere. If that were true, you'd have to be original from your first attempt, and the blank page would be paralysing. If all work is derivative, the bar to begin is low: copy what you love, combine influences nobody else has combined, and a voice emerges from your choices. It's the same picture as [Scenius](/notes/scenius/), where creativity is a mind connected to other minds, and it fits the [Amateur Mindset](/notes/amateur-mindset/): make something now and improve it in increments.

## Where it shows up

Internet memes are the plainest everyday evidence: a template is copied and remixed thousands of times, and the value is in the remixing ([Memes Spread by Copying With Variation](/notes/memes-spread-by-copying-with-variation/)). Paley's film itself depends on the commons. She could build it from other people's sculpture only because that sculpture is out of copyright ([The Public Domain Is Raw Material for New Work](/notes/public-domain-is-raw-material-for-new-work/)). This garden has a small example too: the [Linux parody of Tolkien's Ring Verse](/notes/sources/linux-ring-verse-parody/).

## Two senses of "derivative"

The claim is about the broad sense: every work grows out of a culture. It doesn't mean every work copies a specific earlier one. Critics of the film point out that similar poses across cultures don't prove one sculptor copied another, because shared bodies and conventions produce similar results independently.

The law cares about the narrow sense. That's why [Copyright Protects Expression, Not Ideas](/notes/copyright-protects-expression-not-ideas/): if it protected ideas, the broad borrowing all culture depends on would be illegal.
