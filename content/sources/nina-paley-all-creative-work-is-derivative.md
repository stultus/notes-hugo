## What it shows (paraphrased)

Paley photographed sculptures in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York and sequenced the photographs so that figures from different times and places seem to move, dance and turn into one another. The film contains no imagery of its own. Every frame is someone else's work, so the film demonstrates its claim, that all creative work builds on what came before, just by existing.

## Context

The *Minute Memes* were short films QuestionCopyright.org commissioned to argue that copying drives culture rather than stealing from it; the first was *Copying Is Not Theft*. They're licensed so that anyone may copy and remix them. Paley came to the subject through her feature film *Sita Sings the Blues* (2008), whose release was held up by licensing fees for 1920s songs.

## Criticism

Commenters, among them the archaeologist Kristina Killgrove, pointed out that the film shows the sculptures without context: where and when each was made is lost. Similar poses across cultures don't prove that one sculptor copied another, because shared bodies and shared conventions can produce similar results independently. So the film shows creativity growing out of a culture, not one work copying another.
